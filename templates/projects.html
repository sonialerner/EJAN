Natives on Campus

For two years now, the Environmental Justice Action Network (EJAN) has maintained a garden plot of native D.C. plants at the southern tip of the GW Milken Institute School of Public Health. What began as an entry into a native garden competition has since become a defining element of the network’s identity and social culture. Members enjoy the opportunity to spend time outdoors, care for the garden, connect with one another, and learn about the ecological benefits of native planting in the community. As the garden has flourished, so too has EJAN’s mission—expanding to advocate for native plants across all GW landscaping. The group now collaborates with the Office of Sustainability to promote a stronger native plant presence throughout campus and the broader D.C. area.

Research Initiative

In collaboration with Ward 8 Woods, a nonprofit organization dedicated to reducing trash in Ward 8 forests by employing Ward 8 residents themselves, we are surveying trees in Ward 8 to gather information on trees that are at risk of removal. This project involves travelling to Ward 8 and collecting data and then adding that data to a usable GIS interface
