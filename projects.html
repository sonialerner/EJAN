Policy and Advocacy

Policy and Advocacy works to help students better understand environmental policy while building the skills needed to advocate for meaningful change. By using the unique resources available in Washington, DC, such as policy events, government institutions, nonprofits, and advocacy organizations, we want to gain hands-on experience engaging with the policy process. Our goal is to empower students to turn their environmental interests into informed advocacy and to work toward the policy changes they want to see achieved!

Zero Waste

EJAN's Zero Waste Subcommittee works to reduce campus waste and push GW toward a truly sustainable waste system. We advocate for better waste management, the elimination of single-use plastics, and stronger accountability in how the university handles recycling and compost. We aim to push the university to adopt transparent, effective waste practices, while working with the broader GW community in building a zero-waste future.

Natives on Campus

For two years now, EJAN has maintained a garden plot of native D.C. plants at the southern tip of the GW Milken Institute School of Public Health. What began as an entry into a native garden competition has since become a defining element of the network’s identity and social culture. Members enjoy the opportunity to spend time outdoors, care for the garden, connect with one another, and learn about the ecological benefits of native planting in the community. As the garden has flourished, so too has EJAN’s mission—expanding to advocate for native plants across all GW landscaping. The group now collaborates with the Office of Sustainability to promote a stronger native plant presence throughout campus and the broader D.C. area.

Research Initiative

In collaboration with Ward 8 Woods, a nonprofit organization dedicated to reducing trash in Ward 8 forests by employing Ward 8 residents themselves, we are surveying trees in Ward 8 to gather information on trees that are at risk of removal. This project involves travelling to Ward 8 and collecting data and then adding that data to a usable GIS interface
